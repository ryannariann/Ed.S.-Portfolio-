About this module

The instruction is designed to help 10th-grade English Language Learners and other diverse learners practice one writing skill at a time while working toward the same core learning objectives as their peers.

01

Identify

Find argumentative elements such as a claim, evidence, counterclaim, and rebuttal.

02

Build

Distinguish the parts of an argument and construct a complete claim supported by evidence.

03

Explain

Use reasoning to explain how different elements support your argument at hand.

Learning objectives

By working through the module, learners will practice how to:

  • Identify argumentative writing elements in a text.
  • Distinguish a claim, evidence, reasoning, counterclaim, and rebuttal.
  • Write a complete claim that states a position.
  • Select relevant evidence from a source.
  • Explain in complete sentences how the selected evidence supports the student’s claim.

Instructional Sequence Guide

Module Overview

Complete the five learning modules in order. Each module focuses on one foundational argumentative-writing skill and includes direct access to the existing AdaptEd Student Studio.

1
IDENTIFY

Identify Argumentative Writing Elements

Find the claim, evidence, counterclaim, and rebuttal in an argument.

Success check: I can find the parts of an argument in a text.

2
DISTINGUISH

Distinguish Argumentative Writing Elements

Tell the difference between a claim, evidence, counterclaim, and rebuttal.

Success check: I can explain what each element does and their purpose.

3
CONSTRUCT

Construct Complete Claims

Write one complete sentence that clearly states a position.

Success check: My claim states what I believe or argue.

4
SUPPORT

Support Claims with Relevant Evidence

Choose evidence from a text that supports the claim.

Success check: My evidence connects to my claim and comes from a reliable source.

5
EXPLAIN

Explain How Evidence Supports a Claim

Explain in two or three sentences how the evidence proves or supports the claim.

Success check: My reasoning explains the connection instead of merely repeating the evidence.

Student directions

Learner Directions & Resources

Use these steps as you complete the writing module. Work at a steady pace, read the directions, and use the supports that help you explain your ideas.

Start the Student Studio ↗Open visual resources ↗

Step 1. Complete the baseline task

Before you start the lessons, write a response to the baseline prompt. Try it on your own without the sentence frames or graphic organizers.

Step 2. Complete the five modules in order

Start with IDENTIFY. Then complete DISTINGUISH, CONSTRUCT, SUPPORT, and EXPLAIN. Read each objective and follow the directions on the screen.

Step 3. Use the supports

You may use the color-coded visual guide, vocabulary word bank, sentence frames, and Claim–Evidence–Reasoning organizer. These tools help you organize your thinking.

Step 4. Check your work

Before you submit or move on, ask: Did I answer the question? Did I write a complete sentence? Did I use evidence when asked? Did I explain how the evidence supports my claim?

Step 5. Complete the final task and survey

At the end, complete the final writing task and share feedback about what helped you learn and what could be clearer. Help me, help you!

Quick writing reminders

Claim

My position or answer.

Frame: I believe that ___.

Evidence

Proof from a text or source.

Frame: According to the text, ___.

Reasoning

How the evidence supports your claim.

Frame: This supports my claim because ___.

Counterclaim and rebuttal

The other side and your response to it.

Frames: Some people argue ___. However, ___.

Remember: It is okay to use a sentence starter or word bank while learning. Try to explain your own thinking and ask your teacher for help if a direction is confusing.
